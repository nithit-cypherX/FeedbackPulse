# Explain Concept

Resolve the reader's specific question at the depth they need, rather than teach
everything about the topic. This skill works independently of a repository,
renderer, or another skill.

For Thai explanations, read the reference that fits the requested depth:

- A focused answer or follow-up: [Thai writing examples](references/thai-examples.md).
- A connected lesson, article, or substantial rewrite:
  [Whole-explanation example](references/thai-connected-explanation.md).

Reuse a reference already read and unchanged in the current context. These show
wording and connections, not project facts, mandatory headings, or target length.
Do not expand a short question to fit the longer example.

## Focus on the question

- Lead with the answer and just enough context to identify what it refers to.
  Use the user's language and demonstrated knowledge; do not restart at beginner
  level or recap the whole conversation.
- Read supplied notes or passages before explaining them. If unavailable,
  distinguish a general explanation from an explanation of that source.
- Ask a focused question only when ambiguity would materially change the answer.
  Otherwise, explain immediately using the available context.

## Choose the explanation the question needs

- For "what is it?", give the meaning and the distinction needed to recognize it.
- For "how or why?", explain the relevant mechanism, purpose, or logical
  relationship, making the essential connections explicit.
- For "how do these differ?", compare the points that resolve the confusion.

These are choices, not mandatory sections or an exhaustive taxonomy. Combine
them when the request needs it. A narrow question need not include a definition,
full mechanism, analogy, worked example, and limitations survey.

## Connect the ideas

- Order information for the reader: for example, familiar to unfamiliar,
  process order, or comparison. Make each paragraph contribute to the answer
  and show how a new point connects to what came before. Headings alone do not
  establish those relationships.
- Use plain, concrete language. Name who or what acts, what changes, or what a
  term refers to. Keep useful technical terms, explain unfamiliar ones in context,
  and use consistent names rather than substituting one unfamiliar label for another.
- Use a relevant example when it clarifies the explanation, preferably from the
  current topic. Develop it through the important connections instead of
  introducing unrelated mini-lessons. In a longer explanation, let a question
  raised by the example lead into the next idea; introduce terms when they help
  answer it, not as a glossary before the reader knows why they matter. Add a
  contrasting case when it exposes a meaningful distinction or limit.
- Use an analogy only when helpful; map the relevant parts and state where it
  breaks down. Prefer a direct example when the analogy would distort the idea.

## Preserve accuracy

- Do not invent causes for definitions, mathematical results, statistical
  associations, disputed interpretations, or phenomena with unknown causes.
  Explain the appropriate logical relationship or available evidence instead.
- For formulas, explain needed quantities, units, and assumptions; show how a
  change affects the result when relevant. Include calculations or derivations
  when requested or needed. For algorithms, trace relevant input, state, and
  output; ground complexity claims in the operations involved.
- Mark approximations and their limits. Later detail should refine the earlier
  explanation, not silently contradict it. Identify material discrepancies or
  special conventions in a source.
- Support source-dependent claims with appropriate evidence and distinguish
  uncertainty from established knowledge. Never invent references, experiments,
  or quotations.

## Keep the answer proportionate

Keep the main explanation understandable on its own. Include the details needed
to answer the question correctly; leave optional depth out or separate it when
useful. Keep a necessary condition or important limitation beside the claim it
qualifies, not hidden in optional detail or repeated in every section. Open with
the subject or question, not commentary about how the document is organized.
Expand for a requested lesson or walkthrough, without imposing fixed word
counts, headings, or levels.

Answer in chat unless another artifact is requested. Use prose, lists, code,
tables, or visuals according to what helps the reader. A visual should clarify
a specific relationship that is difficult to follow in words. Diagrams,
collapsible sections, and separate documents are not requirements.

If the user is confused, clarify what the answer refers to and repair the
unclear connection or prerequisite. Change the wording, example, or
representation instead of adding more of the same explanation. Use quizzes,
teach-back, or exercises only when requested or accepted; never withhold an
answer pending an exercise.

Before sending, remove tangents, repeated explanations, and a closing recap
that adds nothing. Replace abstract wording with concrete meaning where the
evidence allows; do not invent specifics to make prose more vivid. Check that
connectors express an actual relationship between ideas. Natural language does
not require jokes, exaggerated praise, or invented personal experience.
Stop when the requested question and its essential
qualifications are covered; do not open another topic just to make the answer
feel comprehensive.
